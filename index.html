Eliza H. Berman

PhD student · NYU Courant Institute

I’m a second-year PhD student in Computer Science at NYU Courant, advised by Daniel Neill and Emily Black. I work on making LLM-assisted decisions more fair and trustworthy by measuring and understanding bias, instability, and unfaithful reasoning in models. I’m grateful to be supported by the NSF Graduate Research Fellowship.

Before NYU, I worked with Carsten Eickhoff at Brown University and the University of Tübingen on LLMs for precision oncology, and spent a year in Peru as a Fulbright English Teaching Assistant. I hold a B.A. in Computer Science and Hispanic Studies from Brown. Outside research, I teach English as a Second Language at an adult night school in New York.

Email Google Scholar GitHub LinkedIn

Research

My work draws on reasoning faithfulness, uncertainty calibration, and mechanistic interpretability to efficiently measure instability in model decisions and reasoning, identify its sources, and assess how it varies across demographic groups. I focus on high-stakes settings where LLMs are increasingly used to inform decisions, such as hiring and scientific research.
